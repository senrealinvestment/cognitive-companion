# Part A — Permission to take part in a research study

*(Map these headings into VCU HRP-502 section order when preparing the official form.)*

## Why am I being invited?

You are invited because you are an adult staff member, faculty member, or volunteer who can take part in an ICU-style **simulation** (practice) session. This is **not** patient care. There are **no real patients** in this study.

## What should I know about a research study?

- Someone will explain this study to you.  
- Whether or not you take part is up to you.  
- You can choose not to take part.  
- You can agree to take part and later change your mind.  
- Your decision will **not** be held against you at work or school.  
- You can ask all the questions you want before you decide.

## Why is this research being done?

We want to learn whether short on-screen “advisory cues” help teams during a crisis simulation, which kinds of cues are useful, how timing works, and whether cues distract. A person (not an AI product) will send the cues by hand. This helps the team decide whether to build more tools later.

## How long will it last and what will I do?

We expect you will be in this study for about **[DURATION]** for one session.

If you say yes, you may:

1. Review this form and ask questions.  
2. Join a short briefing.  
3. Take part in a simulated ICU crisis scenario with your team.  
4. See short text cues on a screen while you work.  
5. Join a short debrief talk.  
6. Fill out a short survey about usefulness and distraction.

Optional recording is described in **Part B**. You can refuse recording and still join the session if the team can run the session without recording **[CONFIRM WITH STUDY TEAM / VCU HRPP]**.

## Is there any way this could be bad for me?

Risks are small and like other sim training:

- You might feel stressed during a crisis scenario.  
- If video or audio is kept, someone might recognize your face or voice.  
- You might worry that your performance could affect your job. **We will not share your individual results with your employer for performance review.**

We lower these risks by limiting who can see the data, using secure local storage, and keeping your choice voluntary.

## Will being in this study help me?

It may not help you directly. You may learn something in the debrief. The main goal is to improve how advisory cues are designed for future education and research.

## What happens if I do not want to be in this research?

You can say no. Nothing bad will happen to your job, grades, evaluations, or training opportunities because you said no.

## What happens if I say yes, but change my mind later?

You can stop at any time. Tell the study team. We will stop collecting new information about you. For recordings already made, see Part B.
